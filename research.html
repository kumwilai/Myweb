Research

What I work on

The through line is the same across twenty five years. A signal is captured, it is damaged by the channel or the sensor, and someone at the far end has to act on it. The question is always which part of the signal has to survive, and how to spend a limited budget protecting it.

Multimedia compression and processing

Where the work began.

Video coding is an exercise in deciding what to discard. My early work looked at what happens when the discarded part matters, through spatial error concealment that rebuilds lost regions from the texture around them, and through rate distortion analysis for multi view coding where several cameras see the same scene. Two United States patents came out of the industrial side of this, on detecting and reducing visual artifacts in digital video.

Multimedia communication over networks

Delivering that signal when the channel does not cooperate.

A wireless channel fades and a network drops packets, and a video stream has to degrade gracefully rather than collapse. This line of work covers cross layer mapping between application quality and network service, fading channel models built from variable length Markov chains, network coding for scalable multicast, and caching policies for content centric networks that decide what to keep near the edge.

Computer vision and image processing

The same question, now about images that a person has to read.

Recent work applies learned models to images where being wrong has a cost. That includes denoising under Poisson noise, defect segmentation with limited labels, crack detection in concrete from depth and graph structure, protective equipment detection on work sites, and retinal image analysis. A continuing interest is that a model trained on a pixel error is not automatically trained on what a specialist actually looks at.

Machine learning and artificial intelligence

Learning where the decision carries consequence.

This covers sign language recognition and generation, which asks a model to read fast articulated motion in the wild, speech enhancement for people who have lost the larynx, rehabilitation exercise assessment, intensive care mortality prediction, and emergency dispatch under risk. What these share is that an error is not a number, it is a person who is not understood or not reached in time.

Collaboration

I supervise graduate students at KMUTT and collaborate with the National Electronics and Computer Technology Center and with industry. If your problem involves a signal that has to survive a lossy path and then be read by a person or a downstream system, it is probably close to something I have worked on.
